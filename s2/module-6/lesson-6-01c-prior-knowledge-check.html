Steps to Write Algebraic Expressions

  1. Identify the Variable: Determine what the variable represents in the real-world situation. It could be anything from the number of items to the distance traveled.

  2. Determine the Operations: Decide which mathematical operations best represent the situation. You might need to add, subtract, multiply, or divide.

  3. Write the Expression: Combine the variable and operations into an algebraic expression.

Understanding Keywords and Phrases

In math, certain words and phrases indicate specific mathematical operations. Recognizing these keywords is crucial for translating descriptions into algebraic expressions.

Keywords for Operations

  • Addition: Words like 'plus,' 'sum,' 'more than,' and 'increased by.'
  • Subtraction: Phrases like 'minus,' 'less,' 'fewer than,' and 'decreased by.'
  • Multiplication: Terms like 'times,' 'multiplied by,' 'product of,' and 'of,' 'each', 'per.'
  • Division: Words such as 'divided by,' 'per,' 'ratio of,' and 'quotient of.'


Writing Algebraic Expressions

Let's practice turning mathematical descriptions into algebraic expressions by focusing on the keywords.


Example 1

Write an algebraic expression for the given statement.

"The sum of a number and 7."

  • We want sum which means addition.
  • We want a number which means a variable and 7.


Example 2

Write an algebraic expression for the given statement.

"15 less than a number."

  • We want less than which means subtraction.
  • We want 15 less than a number (x). Since we want less than, this reverses the order.


Example 3

Write an algebraic expression for the given statement.

"A number decreased by 2, then divided by 4."

  • A number decreased by 2 means subtracting 2 from a variable.
  • Then we want to divide that difference by 4. We must use parenthesis since subtraction does not come before division in the order of operations.
