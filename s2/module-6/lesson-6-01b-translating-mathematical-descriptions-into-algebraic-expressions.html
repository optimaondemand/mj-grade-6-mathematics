💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

Today, we will explore how to write algebraic expressions from mathematical descriptions. An algebraic expression is a mathematical phrase that includes numbers, variables (like x or y), and operation symbols (like +, -, ×, ÷). We previously learned about writing algebraic expressions that represent real-world situations. We also reviewed how to write numerical expressions to represent mathematical descriptions. At times there are keywords and key phrases we can look for to help us determine the operations to be used in the expression. At times, there are some keywords that can be used for more than one operation. So, understanding the context is just as important.

Writing Algebraic Expressions

Let's practice turning mathematical descriptions into algebraic expressions by focusing on the keywords.


Example 1

Write an algebraic expression for the given statement.

"The sum of a number and 7."

  • We want sum which means addition.
  • We want a number which means a variable and 7.


Example 2

Write an algebraic expression for the given statement.

"15 less than a number."

  • We want less than which means subtraction.
  • We want 15 less than a number (x). Since we want less than, this reverses the order.

 

Example 3

Write an algebraic expression for the given statement.

"Three times a number, increased by 5."

  • Three times a number means multiplying 3 and a variable.
  • Increased by 5 means to add 5 to that previous expression.

Conclusion

Writing algebraic expressions from mathematical descriptions is like translating a language. By identifying the keywords and understanding what they represent, you can turn words into mathematical phrases. Remember, understanding the order of operations is crucial when dealing with multiple operations. Keep practicing, and you'll become fluent in this important aspect of algebra!
