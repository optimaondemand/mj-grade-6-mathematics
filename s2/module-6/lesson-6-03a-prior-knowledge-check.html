📖 PRIOR KNOWLEDGE

In previous grades and lessons, you learned how to compare two numbers using the inequality symbols for less than, greater than, or equal to. As we get ready to understand how to write algebraic inequalities representing written descriptions, it is important to understand what the inequality symbols mean and how they are used to compare values. These symbols help us understand the relationship between different numbers, including integers (whole numbers) and decimals, whether they are positive or negative. Let's dive into how we can use these symbols to compare numbers.


Understanding Inequality Symbols

First, let's review the inequality symbols we'll be using:

  • Greater Than (>) means the number on the left is larger than the number on the right.
  • Less Than (<) means the number on the left is smaller than the number on the right.
  • Greater Than or Equal To (≥) means the number on the left is larger than or equal to the number on the right.
  • Less Than or Equal To (≤) means the number on the left is smaller than or equal to the number on the right.


Comparing Numbers

When comparing numbers, always look at their value relative to zero.

  • Remember, positive numbers are greater than negative numbers.
  • Among negative numbers, the one closer to zero is greater.


Comparing a Negative Number with a Negative Number

  • Example 1: Compare -3 and -5.

    • Since -3 is closer to zero than -5, -3 is greater than -5.
    • We write this as -3 > -5.

  • Example 2: Compare -2.5 and -3.4.

    • Since -2.5 is closer to zero than -3.4, -2.5 is greater than -3.4.
    • We write this as -2.5 > -3.4.

 

Comparing a Positive Number with a Negative Number

  • Example 3: Compare 2 and -4.

    • Since 2 is a positive number and -4 is a negative number, 2 is greater than -4.
    • We write this as 2 > -4.

  • Example 4: Compare -1.2 and 0.8.

    • Since -1.2 is negative and 0.8 is positive, 0.8 is greater than -1.2.
    • We write this as 0.8 > -1.2.

 

Tips for Comparing Numbers

  • Always remember that any positive number is greater than any negative number.
  • When comparing two negative numbers, the one that is less negative (closer to zero) is the greater number.
  • Use a number line in your mind or on paper to visualize the positions of the numbers. This can make it easier to compare them.


Conclusion

Comparing integers and decimals, whether positive or negative, is a fundamental skill in math. By understanding and using inequality symbols correctly, you can easily determine the relationship between different numbers. Keep practicing with these examples, and soon you'll be able to compare any numbers with confidence!
