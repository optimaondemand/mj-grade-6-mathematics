📚 VOCABULARY

Additive Inverse Property: The property that states for every number a, there is a number −a such that a+(−a)=0.

Addition Property of Equality: The property that states if you add the same number to both sides of an equation, the two sides remain equal.

Associative Property: The property that states the way numbers are grouped in addition or multiplication does not change their sum or product.

Commutative Property: The property that states the order in which two numbers are added or multiplied does not change their sum or product.

Division Property of Equality: The property that states if you divide both sides of an equation by the same nonzero number, the two sides remain equal.

Equation: A mathematical statement that shows two expressions are equal, using an equal sign =.

Identity Property: The property that states the sum of any number and 0 is the number itself, and the product of any number and 1 is the number itself.

Integer: A whole number that can be positive, negative, or zero.

Inverse Operations: Operations that undo each other, such as addition and subtraction or multiplication and division.

Multiplicative Inverse: The number that, when multiplied by a given number, results in a product of 1.

Multiplication Property of Equality: The property that states if you multiply both sides of an equation by the same number, the two sides remain equal.

Number Line: A straight, horizontal line with numbers placed at equal intervals along its length, used to represent numbers and their relationships.

Substitution Property: The property that states if two things are equal, you can substitute one for the other in an equation or expression.

Solution: A value that makes an equation true when substituted for the variable.

Variable: A symbol, usually a letter, that represents an unknown value in an equation or expression.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to solve one-step linear equations that involve addition or subtraction. We will learn about additive inverses and how to use them as opposite operations to isolate the variable and find the solution. Understanding these concepts will help you solve equations accurately and with confidence.


Understanding Additive Inverses

An additive inverse is a number that, when added to a given number, results in zero. In other words, the additive inverse of a number is its opposite.

Example: the additive inverse of 7 is -7, and the additive inverse of -5 is 5.

**Using additive inverses, we can isolate the variable in an equation to solve for its value.
