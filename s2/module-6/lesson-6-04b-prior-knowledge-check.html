Understanding Algebraic Inequalities

An algebraic inequality tells us how numbers relate to each other. Instead of saying two numbers are exactly the same, an inequality shows if one number is greater than, less than, greater than or equal to, or less than or equal to another number. We use variables like "x" to represent our mystery number and symbols like ">", "<", "≥", "≤" to show the relationship between that number and others.

Symbols and Their Meanings:

  • ">" means greater than
  • "<" means less than
  • "≥" means greater than or equal to
  • "≤" means less than or equal to

How to Represent Inequalities on a Number Line

Open Circle:

  • Use an open circle when your inequality is either ">" (greater than) or "<" (less than).
  • What it means: The number at the open circle is not included in the solution. It's like saying, "You can get close, but you can't be exactly this number."
  • Example: If your inequality is "x > 3," you'd place an open circle at 3 on the number line and shade everything to the right, indicating that x can be any number greater than 3 but not 3 itself.

Closed Circle:

  • Use a closed circle when your inequality includes "≥" (greater than or equal to) or "≤" (less than or equal to).
  • What it means: The number where you've drawn the closed circle is included in the solution. This is like saying, "This number and anything beyond it (to the right for ≥ or to the left for ≤) works for us."
  • Example: If your inequality is "x ≤ -2," you'd put a closed circle at -2 on the number line and shade everything to the left, showing that x can be -2 or any number less than -2.
