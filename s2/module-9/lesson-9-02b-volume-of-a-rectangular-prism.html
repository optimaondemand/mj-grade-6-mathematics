📚 VOCABULARY

Area: The amount of space inside a flat shape, measured in square units.

Cube: A three-dimensional shape with six equal square faces.

Dimension: A measurement of length, width, or height of a shape or object.

Model: A representation or example of a shape or object used to help understand math concepts.

Net: A flat, two-dimensional pattern that can be folded to form a three-dimensional shape.

Rectangular Prism: A three-dimensional shape with six faces, where all faces are rectangles.

Rectangular Pyramid: A three-dimensional shape with a rectangular base and triangular faces that meet at a single point.

Slant Height: The height of the slanted side of a pyramid, measured from the base to the top along the surface.

Surface Area: The total area of all the faces or surfaces of a three-dimensional shape.

Volume: The amount of space inside a three-dimensional shape, measured in cubic units.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to find the volume of a rectangular prism using its length, width, and height. A rectangular prism is a three-dimensional shape with six faces, and all of its faces are rectangles. The volume of a prism tells us how much space is inside it, or how many cubic units it can hold. In the last lesson, we learned how to find the volume by using cubes to model the space inside a prism. Today, we’ll learn a faster way to calculate the volume using a formula and measurements like length, width, and height.


Understanding the Volume Formula

To find the volume of a rectangular prism, we use the following formula:

\(\text{Volume} = \text{length} \cdot \text{width} \cdot \text{height} \)

This formula works because the length, width, and height tell us how many cubes fit along each dimension of the prism. By multiplying them together, we can figure out how many cubes fill the entire prism.
