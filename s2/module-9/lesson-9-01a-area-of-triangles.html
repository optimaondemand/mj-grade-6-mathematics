📚 VOCABULARY

Area: The amount of space inside a shape, measured in square units.

Base: The bottom side of a shape, or one of its sides used to help find the area.

Composite Figure: A shape made by combining two or more simple shapes.

Decomposing: Breaking a shape down into smaller, simpler shapes to make it easier to work with.

Height: The distance from the top to the bottom of a shape, measured straight up and down.

Parallelogram: A four-sided shape with opposite sides that are parallel and equal in length.

Quadrilateral: A shape that has four sides and four angles.

Rectangle: A four-sided shape with opposite sides that are equal and all angles are right angles (90 degrees).

Trapezoid: A four-sided shape with one pair of parallel sides.

Triangle: A shape with three sides and three angles.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to find the area of a triangle. You may already know how to find the area of a rectangle by multiplying its length by its width. But what about triangles? Triangles are different shapes, but they share a special connection with rectangles. By understanding this connection, we can find an easy way to calculate the area of a triangle using a formula. By the end of this lesson, you’ll not only understand how to use the formula for the area of a triangle but also how this formula comes from what you already know about rectangles!

Triangles That Aren’t Right Triangles

So far, we’ve talked about triangles that might look like one-half of a rectangle, which are right triangles. But what about triangles that don’t have a 90-degree angle, like the one shown in the images? These triangles can still be solved using the same formula, but it’s important to know where to find the height.

Step 1: Identify the Base

Just like with any triangle, you start by identifying the base, which can be any side of the triangle. Pick one side of the triangle to be your base. Let’s say it’s the bottom side.

Step 2: Find the Height

Here’s where things change with triangles that aren’t right triangles. The height is the distance from the highest point of the triangle straight down to the base, but it may not be one of the triangle's sides. For some triangles, the height will be inside the triangle, like in the images, where the highest point connects to the base at a right angle inside the triangle. For others, the height might be outside the triangle, and you’ll need to draw a line extending from the top of the triangle down to the base.

**NOTE** In both cases, you measure this distance, the height, just like before—it’s the perpendicular distance from the top of the triangle to the base.

Step 3: Use the Formula

Once you know the base and the height, the rest is easy. You use the same formula as before .

The base = 9 feet (ft) and the height = 6 feet (ft).
