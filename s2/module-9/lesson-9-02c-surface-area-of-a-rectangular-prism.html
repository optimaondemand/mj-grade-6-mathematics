📚 VOCABULARY

Area: The amount of space inside a flat shape, measured in square units.

Cube: A three-dimensional shape with six equal square faces.

Dimension: A measurement of length, width, or height of a shape or object.

Model: A representation or example of a shape or object used to help understand math concepts.

Net: A flat, two-dimensional pattern that can be folded to form a three-dimensional shape.

Rectangular Prism: A three-dimensional shape with six faces, where all faces are rectangles.

Rectangular Pyramid: A three-dimensional shape with a rectangular base and triangular faces that meet at a single point.

Slant Height: The height of the slanted side of a pyramid, measured from the base to the top along the surface.

Surface Area: The total area of all the faces or surfaces of a three-dimensional shape.

Volume: The amount of space inside a three-dimensional shape, measured in cubic units.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore what a net of a rectangular prism is and how we can use it to find the surface area of the prism. A net is a two-dimensional pattern that can be folded to make a three-dimensional shape. For a rectangular prism, the net shows all the faces of the prism laid out flat. By understanding the net, we can easily calculate the surface area, which is the total area of all the faces of the prism added together. By the end of this lesson, you’ll know how to use a net to find the surface area of a rectangular prism.


What Is a Net?

A net is like an unfolded version of a three-dimensional shape. When you unfold the rectangular prism, you can see all six of its faces, which are rectangles. In the first image, you can see a rectangular prism along with its net, which shows the six rectangular faces laid flat.

Each face of the prism has a specific length and width, and by looking at the net, you can clearly see the size of each face. These dimensions are important for finding the surface area because surface area is the total space that covers the outside of the prism.

Conclusion

By understanding the net of a rectangular prism, we can easily find the surface area by calculating the area of each face and adding them together.
