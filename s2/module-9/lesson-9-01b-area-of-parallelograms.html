📚 VOCABULARY

Area: The amount of space inside a shape, measured in square units.

Base: The bottom side of a shape, or one of its sides used to help find the area.

Composite Figure: A shape made by combining two or more simple shapes.

Decomposing: Breaking a shape down into smaller, simpler shapes to make it easier to work with.

Height: The distance from the top to the bottom of a shape, measured straight up and down.

Parallelogram: A four-sided shape with opposite sides that are parallel and equal in length.

Quadrilateral: A shape that has four sides and four angles.

Rectangle: A four-sided shape with opposite sides that are equal and all angles are right angles (90 degrees).

Trapezoid: A four-sided shape with one pair of parallel sides.

Triangle: A shape with three sides and three angles.

Decomposing a Parallelogram

A parallelogram may look tricky at first because of its slanted sides, but it’s actually just a combination of a rectangle and a triangle. To understand this, imagine you cut off a slanted triangle from one side of the parallelogram and move it to the other side. When you do this, the parallelogram turns into a rectangle!

Here’s how it works:

  1. Cutting off the triangle: You can imagine drawing a vertical line from the top corner of the parallelogram down to the base, cutting off the slanted part. This slanted part is a triangle.
  2. Moving the triangle: Once you have cut off the triangle, you move it to the opposite side of the parallelogram. Now, instead of having a slanted shape, you have a rectangle.
    This shows that the area of the parallelogram is the same as the area of the rectangle you created.

Conclusion

Finding the area of a parallelogram becomes simple when you break it down into shapes you already know, like rectangles and triangles.
