📚 VOCABULARY

Area: The amount of space inside a flat shape, measured in square units.

Cube: A three-dimensional shape with six equal square faces.

Dimension: A measurement of length, width, or height of a shape or object.

Model: A representation or example of a shape or object used to help understand math concepts.

Net: A flat, two-dimensional pattern that can be folded to form a three-dimensional shape.

Rectangular Prism: A three-dimensional shape with six faces, where all faces are rectangles.

Rectangular Pyramid: A three-dimensional shape with a rectangular base and triangular faces that meet at a single point.

Slant Height: The height of the slanted side of a pyramid, measured from the base to the top along the surface.

Surface Area: The total area of all the faces or surfaces of a three-dimensional shape.

Volume: The amount of space inside a three-dimensional shape, measured in cubic units.

A pyramid is a three-dimensional shape that has a base and triangular faces that come together at a single point, called the apex. The net of a rectangular pyramid is a two-dimensional shape that, when folded, forms the pyramid. By the end of this lesson, you’ll understand how to use the net of a rectangular pyramid to calculate its surface area.


What Is a Net?

A net is the flat, unfolded version of a three-dimensional shape. For a rectangular pyramid, the net shows the rectangular base and the four triangular faces laid out flat. Each triangle is connected to one side of the rectangle, and when you fold the net along the edges, it forms the three-dimensional pyramid.

In the images provided, you can see the net of a rectangular pyramid. Notice how the base of the pyramid is a rectangle, and the triangles are connected to the rectangle’s sides. Each triangle represents one of the slanted sides of the pyramid.
