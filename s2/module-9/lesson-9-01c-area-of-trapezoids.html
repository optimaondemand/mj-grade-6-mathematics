📚 VOCABULARY

Area: The amount of space inside a shape, measured in square units.

Base: The bottom side of a shape, or one of its sides used to help find the area.

Composite Figure: A shape made by combining two or more simple shapes.

Decomposing: Breaking a shape down into smaller, simpler shapes to make it easier to work with.

Height: The distance from the top to the bottom of a shape, measured straight up and down.

Parallelogram: A four-sided shape with opposite sides that are parallel and equal in length.

Quadrilateral: A shape that has four sides and four angles.

Rectangle: A four-sided shape with opposite sides that are equal and all angles are right angles (90 degrees).

Trapezoid: A four-sided shape with one pair of parallel sides.

Triangle: A shape with three sides and three angles.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to find the area of a trapezoid by breaking it down into simpler shapes. A trapezoid is a four-sided figure with one pair of parallel sides. By decomposing a trapezoid into triangles and rectangles, we can easily calculate its area. We will also learn how to combine the areas of the different parts to find the total area of the trapezoid. By the end of this lesson, you’ll know how to break down any trapezoid and use a formula to find its area.

Conclusion

By breaking down a trapezoid into triangles and a rectangle, you can easily find its area. The area of a trapezoid is the sum of the areas of its simpler shapes.
