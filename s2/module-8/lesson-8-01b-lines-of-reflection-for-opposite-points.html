📚 VOCABULARY

Axes: The axes are like two straight paths that cross each other on a flat surface called the coordinate plane. One path is flat (horizontal) and is called the x-axis, and the other path stands up (vertical) and is called the y-axis. They meet in the middle at a point called the origin.

Coordinate Plane: Imagine a flat playground divided by two lines that cross each other right in the middle: one line goes from left to right (x-axis), and the other line goes from up to down (y-axis). This playground is called the coordinate plane, and it's used for finding specific spots (points) by walking along these lines.

Coordinate: A coordinate is like a clue that tells you where to find a treasure on a map. It uses two numbers: one tells you how many steps to take right or left from the center, and the other tells you how many steps to go up or down. This clue helps you find exactly where the treasure (point) is on the map (coordinate plane).

Ordered Pair: An ordered pair is a set of two numbers that work together to show a point's location on the coordinate plane. It's like instructions for a game: the first number (x) tells you how many spaces to move left or right, and the second number (y) tells you how many spaces to move up or down. The numbers are always shown in a specific order, inside parentheses, like this: (x, y).

Origin: The origin is the starting point on the coordinate plane where the x-axis and y-axis cross each other. It's like the "home base" in a game, where you start before moving to other spots. The coordinates of the origin are always (0, 0), because you don't have to move left, right, up, or down to get there from itself.

Quadrant: The coordinate plane is split into four sections by the x-axis and y-axis, like slicing a pizza into four pieces. Each section is called a quadrant and is numbered from 1 to 4. Quadrant 1 is the top right piece, and then they go around in a circle counterclockwise. Each quadrant helps us describe where points are on the plane, like telling which piece of the pizza you want.

Reflection: Reflection is like looking in a mirror. When you reflect a point across one of the lines on the coordinate plane (either the x-axis or y-axis), it flips to the opposite side, just like how your mirror image flips left to right. It's a way to create a mirror image of shapes or points on the plane.

Symmetry: Symmetry is when something has two sides that are exactly the same, like opening a book and seeing each side mirror the other. On the coordinate plane, if you can draw a line (like the x-axis or y-axis) that makes the two halves of a shape look exactly alike, then that shape has symmetry. It's like having a perfect balance, where both sides match up perfectly.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore the concept of reflection across the axes on the coordinate plane. Reflection is like using a mirror to flip a point over a line, such as the x-axis or y-axis, which results in a mirror image of the point on the opposite side of that line. Understanding how reflection changes a point's coordinates is a key skill in geometry that allows us to visualize and solve problems involving symmetry and transformation. Let's dive into how reflecting a point over an axis affects its coordinates and how to determine the new location of a reflected point.


Reflecting Over the X-axis:

  • When a point is reflected over the x-axis, its x-coordinate remains the same, but its y-coordinate changes sign.
  • Example: Reflecting the point (3, 4) over the x-axis.
    • Original coordinates: (3, 4)
    • After reflection: (3, -4)
  • This means the point has moved vertically across the x-axis to the mirror position.


Reflecting Over the Y-axis:

  • When a point is reflected over the y-axis, its y-coordinate stays the same, but its x-coordinate changes sign.
  • Example: Reflecting the point (3, 4) over the y-axis.
    • Original coordinates: (3, 4)
    • After reflection: (-3, 4)
  • This indicates the point has moved horizontally across the y-axis to the opposite side.

Example 3

If we reflect the point (-5, -6) over the x-axis, in which quadrant will the point be?

  • After reflection, the point (-5, 6) would be in Quadrant II because the y-coordinate becomes positive while the x-coordinate remains negative.



Conclusion

Reflection is a powerful tool in geometry that allows us to manipulate points on the coordinate plane by "flipping" them across the axes. By changing the sign of the appropriate coordinate (x-coordinate when reflecting over the y-axis and y-coordinate when reflecting over the x-axis), we can easily find the mirror image of a point. Understanding how to reflect points and predict their new locations helps us grasp concepts of symmetry, which are fundamental in mathematics and the world around us. Whether it's creating art, designing buildings, or solving puzzles, reflection, and symmetry play key roles in bringing balance and harmony to our creations.
