In our previous lessons, we've explored plotting points and navigating the coordinate plane. Today, we will focus on a practical application of geometry: calculating the area and perimeter of rectangles and triangles. Understanding these calculations helps us solve real-world problems, such as finding out how much material is needed for a project or determining the length of fencing to enclose a garden. Let's break down the steps for finding the area and perimeter of these two common shapes. In the next lesson, we will be exploring rectangles and triangles on the coordinate plane and using distance to find the lengths of the sides to calculate their area or perimeter.


Calculating the Area and Perimeter of a Rectangle

A rectangle is a four-sided shape where every angle is a right angle, and opposite sides are equal. The area of a rectangle is found by multiplying the length and the width, while the perimeter is the total distance around the edges or sides of the rectangle.


Example for Rectangle:

  • Dimensions: Length = 12.5 meters, Width = 7 meters
  • Area Formula: Area = Length × Width
    • Calculation: Area = 12.5 m × 7 m = 87.5 square meters =
  • Perimeter Formula: Perimeter = 2 × (Length + Width)
    • Calculation: Perimeter = 2 × (12.5 m + 7 m) = 39 meters = 39 m



Calculating the Area and Perimeter of a Triangle

A triangle is a shape with three sides and three angles. The area of a triangle can be calculated if you know the base and the height. The perimeter is found by adding up the lengths of all three sides.


Example for Triangle:

  • Dimensions: Base = 10 meters, Height = 6 meters, Side 2 = 8 meters, Side 3 = 7 meters
  • Area Formula: Area = 0.5 × Base × Height
    • Calculation: Area = 0.5 × 10 m × 6 m = 30 square meters =
  • Perimeter Formula: Perimeter = Base + Side 2 + Side 3
    • Calculation: Perimeter = 10 m + 8 m + 7 m = 25 meters = 25 m



Understanding the Formulas

  • Rectangle Area: The formula considers the full coverage of the space within the rectangle's boundaries by multiplying the length and the width.
  • Rectangle Perimeter: This calculation sums the boundaries of the rectangle, ensuring the entire outer edge is accounted for.
  • Triangle Area: The formula reflects half the product of base and height, representing the space covered by the triangular shape.
  • Triangle Perimeter: Adding all sides gives the total distance around the triangle, which is useful for applications like fencing.


Conclusion

Calculating the area and perimeter of rectangles and triangles allows us to apply our mathematical skills to understand and manage the space around us better. Whether measuring land, fabric, or fencing, mastering these formulas equips us with the tools to tackle everyday challenges efficiently. By practicing these calculations, you can enhance your problem-solving abilities and deepen your understanding of geometric concepts.
