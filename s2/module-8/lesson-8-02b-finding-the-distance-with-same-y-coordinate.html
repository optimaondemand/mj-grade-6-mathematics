📚 VOCABULARY

Absolute Value: It is like measuring how far a number is from zero on a number line, no matter which direction. If you think of it like a distance, it's always a positive number or zero. For example, the absolute value of both -5 and 5 is 5, because each is 5 steps away from zero.

Axes: They are the two crossing lines on a graph that help us locate points. The horizontal line is called the x-axis, and the vertical line is called the y-axis. They meet at a point called the origin. You can think of them like the lines on a map that help you find a treasure.

Coordinates: They tell you the exact location of a point on a graph or map. They are like a set of directions that help you find a point. Coordinates are usually written as two numbers in parentheses, like (3, 4), where the first number tells how far to move right or left, and the second number tells how far to move up or down.

Coordinate Plane: This is a flat surface on which we draw grids to plot points. It is made up of the x-axis (the horizontal line) and the y-axis (the vertical line), which divide the plane into four sections called quadrants. It's like a big piece of graph paper where you can show where things are.

Horizontal Distance: It is how far two points are from each other left to right (along the x-axis) on a graph. Imagine you're walking straight along a hallway without turning; the number of steps you take from start to finish is the horizontal distance.

Ordered Pair: It is a set of two numbers used to describe the location of a point on a graph. It's like an address for points. The first number tells you where to find the point left or right along the x-axis, and the second number tells you where the point is up or down along the y-axis.

Origin: The starting point on a graph where the x-axis and y-axis cross each other. It's always at point (0, 0). You can think of it as the "home base" on a game board, where you start your journey.

Quadrant: A single section that is one of the four sections of the coordinate plane. They are numbered as Quadrant I, Quadrant II, Quadrant III, and Quadrant IV. Starting from the upper right section (Quadrant I) and going around counterclockwise, each quadrant helps us describe where points are on the plane.

Vertical Distance: It is how far two points are apart up and down (along the y-axis) on a graph. Imagine you're climbing or descending a ladder; the number of rungs you pass is the vertical distance.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to measure the horizontal distance between two points on the coordinate plane, specifically when these points share the same y-coordinate. This concept is essential for understanding how to calculate distances across a flat surface, such as measuring the length of a wall or the distance between two objects lying on the same horizontal level. By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to find this distance using both a graph and algebraic calculations.


What is Horizontal Distance on a Coordinate Plane?

Horizontal distance refers to the space between two points that lie directly beside each other on the coordinate plane, either to the left or right. This distance measures how far apart these points are side-to-side. Here’s how you can find it:

  • Points with the Same Y-Value: When two points have the same y-coordinate, they are aligned horizontally. To find the horizontal distance:
    • Using a Graph: Plot both points on the coordinate plane and draw a line horizontally from one point to the other. The length of this line represents the horizontal distance.
    • Using Algebra: Subtract the x-coordinate of one point from the x-coordinate of the other point. The absolute value of this difference gives the horizontal distance, ensuring it is always a positive measurement.

Problem 1

Find the horizontal distance between the points (2, 5) and (8, 5) using the graph shown.

  • Solution with a Graph:
    • Step 1: Draw a horizontal line connecting the two points. Since both points share the y-coordinate of 5, the line will be perfectly flat.
    • Step 2: Count the units of the length of the line, which is the difference between the x-values: 8 - 2 = 6 units. So, the horizontal distance is 6 units.


Problem 2

Calculate the horizontal distance between the points (4.5, 3) and (1.5, 3) using algebra.

  • Solution with Algebra:
    • Step 1: Identify the x-values of both points, which are 4.5 and 1.5, respectively.
    • Step 2: Subtract the smaller x-value from the larger x-value to find the distance: 4.5 - 1.5 = 3.
    • Step 3: The absolute value of 3 is 3, so the horizontal distance is 3 units.


Problem 3

Find the horizontal distance between the points (-3, 7) and (4, 7) using a graph.

  • Solution with a Graph:
    • Step 1: Plot both points on the coordinate plane. Both points lie at the same vertical height (7 units up from the origin) but are positioned on different sides of the y-axis (-3 units to the left and 4 units to the right).
    • Step 2: Draw a horizontal line connecting the two points. The line will span from the left side of the y-axis to the right, crossing the y-axis at the origin.
    • Step 3: Measure the length of the line, which is the sum of the absolute values of the x-values because they are on opposite sides of the y-axis: |-3| + |4| = 3 + 4 = 7 units. So, the horizontal distance is 7 units.


Problem 4

Calculate the horizontal distance between the points (-6, 2) and (-2, 2) using algebra.

  • Solution with Algebra:
    • Step 1: Identify the x-values of both points, which are -6 and -2, respectively, both on the left side of the y-axis.
    • Step 2: Subtract the more negative x-value from the less negative x-value to find the distance: -2 - (-6) = -2 + 6 = 4.
    • Step 3: The absolute value of 4 is 4, so the horizontal distance is 4 units.
