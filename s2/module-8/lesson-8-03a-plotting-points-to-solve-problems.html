📚 VOCABULARY

Area: The amount of space inside a shape, like the inside of a rectangle or circle. It's like counting how many square tiles it would take to completely cover a floor without any gaps.

Axes: The two lines that cross each other to form a coordinate plane. One is horizontal and called the x-axis, and the other is vertical and called the y-axis. They meet at a point called the origin.

Coordinates: Numbers that tell you the location of a point on a graph. The first number tells you how far to move right or left on the x-axis, and the second number tells you how far to move up or down on the y-axis.

Coordinate Plane: A flat surface where you can draw points, lines, and shapes using coordinates. It's divided into four parts by the axes, helping you describe exactly where things are placed.

Ordered Pair: A pair of numbers used to show the position of a point on the coordinate plane. The first number is for the x-axis, and the second is for the y-axis.

Origin: The starting point on the coordinate plane where the x-axis and y-axis cross. It's like the center point of a map and is always at (0, 0).

Perimeter: The total distance around the edge of a shape, like adding up all the sides of a triangle or a square. It's like walking all the way around a fence to measure how much fencing is needed.

Quadrant: One of the four sections of the coordinate plane, created by the crossing of the x-axis and y-axis. Each section is numbered from 1 to 4, starting from the upper right and moving counterclockwise.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to solve real-world problems by plotting points on the coordinate plane and using vertical and horizontal distances. This skill is incredibly useful for everything from navigating maps to designing layouts and solving geometrical problems. We'll look at how to determine the location of a place given its distance from another location, find the distance between two places, and even how to find the location of the fourth vertex of a rectangle given the other three vertices. By understanding these concepts, you can apply mathematical reasoning to visualize and solve practical problems.


Using Vertical and Horizontal Distances

To begin, remember that the coordinate plane is divided into four quadrants with values along both the x-axis (horizontal) and y-axis (vertical).


Problem 1

Finding a Location from a Given Point

  • Given: The location of a park is at (5, -10), and a library is located 15 units east and 8 units north of the park.
  • Solution:
    • Start at (5, -10).
    • Move 15 units east or 15 units right (increase x by 15): 5 + 15 = 20.
    • Move 8 units north or 8 units up (increase y by 8): -10 + 8 = -2.
  • Resulting Coordinates of the Library: (20, -2).


Problem 2

Calculating Distance Between Two Locations

  • Given: The coordinates of a coffee shop at (22, -5) and a bookstore at (12, 7). If you walk the sidewalk to get from the coffee shop to the bookstore, how far will you walk?
  • Solution:
    • Vertical Distance: |-5 - 7| = | -12 | = 12 units.
    • Horizontal Distance: |22 - 12| = 10 units.
  • We will walk a total of 22 units to get to the bookstore. 12 units north and 10 units west.



Problem 3

Finding the Fourth Vertex of a Rectangle

  • Given: Coordinates of three vertices: A(4, 5), B(4, -6), and C(-6, 5).
  • Solution:
    • Recognize that A and B share the same x-coordinate, and A and C share the same y-coordinate.
    • Since rectangles have parallel sides, the missing vertex D will share the same x-coordinate with C and the same y-coordinate with B.
    • Coordinates of D: (-6, -10).



Applications of Plotting and Distance Calculation

By solving these example problems, you can see how understanding plotting points and distances in all four quadrants of the coordinate plane is crucial:

  • Navigating Maps: By plotting points and calculating distances, you can determine the best routes or find specific locations relative to each other.
  • Designing Layouts: Whether setting up a room or planning an event space, understanding how to plot points helps in visualizing and utilizing the space effectively.
  • Solving Geometric Problems: From finding missing points in shapes to calculating area and perimeter, the skills learned are broadly applicable.


Conclusion

Today's lesson provided practical applications for the mathematical skills of plotting points and calculating distances on the coordinate plane. Understanding these concepts not only enhances your problem-solving abilities but also equips you with the tools to approach and solve real-world problems effectively. With practice, these skills will become second nature, enabling you to confidently tackle various challenges both in academics and everyday life.
