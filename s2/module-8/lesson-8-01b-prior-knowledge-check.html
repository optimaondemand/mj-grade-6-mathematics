📖 PRIOR KNOWLEDGE

In a previous lesson, we learned about plotting points on the coordinate plane and identifying what quadrant a point lies in. As we get ready to explore reflections of points over the x- or y-axis, it is important to review plotting points and the quadrants of the coordinate plane. We use the quadrants to help identify where a point is on the coordinate plane. We use the coordinates of the ordered pair to help identify how to get to that location. Let's review what this means.


The Coordinate Plane

  • The coordinate plane is a flat surface that is divided by two lines: the x-axis and the y-axis.
  • The point where they meet is super special and is called the origin, and marked with the coordinates (0, 0).
  • The x-axis goes left to right containing the positive x values to the right and the negative x values to the left. The y-axis goes up and down containing the positive y values up and the negative y values down.
  • The axes divide the plane into four sections called quadrants. They are numbered in a counterclockwise direction, starting from the top right. Each is labeled numerically from I to IV.
    • Quadrant I (1): This is the top right section of the plane. It's like the first piece of pie if we cut the plane into four slices starting from the top right and going counter-clockwise. Here, both the x and y values of points are positive.
    • Quadrant II (2): Moving counter-clockwise, this is the top left section. Imagine it as the second piece of the pie. In this quadrant, x values are negative, but y values are positive.
    • Quadrant III (3): This is the bottom left section, the third piece of our pie. Here, both the x and y values of points are negative.
    • Quadrant IV (4): The last section is the bottom right. It's like the last piece of our pie. In this quadrant, x values are positive, but y values are negative.


Ordered Pairs or Coordinate Points

An ordered pair, (x, y), consists of two elements where 'x' represents the horizontal displacement from the origin along the x-axis, and 'y' represents the vertical displacement along the y-axis. The sign of each value indicates direction: positive values denote movement to the right (for x) or upward (for y), while negative values indicate movement to the left (for x) or downward (for y).

Breaking Down an Ordered Pair: Every point on the coordinate plane is given as an ordered pair, like (x, y), where x tells you how far to move right or left from the origin, and y tells you how far to move up or down.

  • If x is a positive number, move right from the origin; if it's negative, move left.
  • If y is positive, move up from where you are; if it's negative, move down.

To plot a point on the coordinate plane, one begins at the origin.

  • The first number of the ordered pair directs how far to move horizontally, and the second number specifies the vertical movement.
    • For example, to plot the point (3, -2), move 3 units right from the origin along the x-axis and then 2 units down along the y-axis.


Identifying the Quadrant

The quadrant in which a point lies is determined by the signs of its coordinates. Points with positive x and y values (x, y) are in Quadrant I, points with negative x and positive y values (-x, y) are in Quadrant II, points with negative x and y values (-x, -y) are in Quadrant III, and points with positive x and negative y values (x, -y) are in Quadrant IV.

For instance:

  • A point plotted at (2, 3) lies in Quadrant I, indicating both x and y are positive, moving right and up from the origin.
  • The coordinates (-4, 5) position a point in Quadrant II, showing a left and upward movement, reflecting a negative x and a positive y.
  • Plotting a point given coordinates (-3, -4) places it in Quadrant III, where both x and y are negative, indicating left and downward movement.
  • A point at (5, -6) is found in Quadrant IV, with a positive x and negative y, demonstrating right and downward movement from the origin.

Given the coordinates (-5, 6), you'd know to move 5 steps to the left (since it's negative) and 6 steps up. This would place you in Quadrant II because all points with a negative x-coordinate and a positive y-coordinate fall into this section.

Conclusion

The coordinate plane is a fantastic tool that mathematicians, geographers, architects, and many others use to precisely describe locations. It’s like a map that helps us find treasure, understand geometry, and solve puzzles. By learning how to navigate this map, using axes to determine our movements, and understanding quadrants to describe locations, we become explorers of our mathematical world. Whether we’re plotting points or finding out where they are, we're learning the language of the coordinate plane. Understanding the coordinate plane and its components—axes, origin, quadrants, and the method of plotting points through ordered pairs—is crucial for navigating and representing spatial relationships mathematically. This foundational knowledge enables precise communication and problem-solving within various mathematical contexts.
