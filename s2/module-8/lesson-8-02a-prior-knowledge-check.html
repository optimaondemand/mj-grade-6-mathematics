📖 PRIOR KNOWLEDGE

In a previous lesson, we learned how to plot points on the coordinate plane using ordered pairs called coordinates. In this lesson, we will be focusing on what happens when we change only the y-values while keeping the x-value the same. This specific way of moving along the coordinate plane shows us how to move vertically, either up or down along the y-axis. Understanding how to manipulate the y-coordinate while keeping the x-coordinate constant is crucial for navigating this two-dimensional space effectively and will help us visualize data, solve problems, and understand patterns. In the next lesson, we will be exploring finding the distance between two points that have the same x-value. Understanding the vertical movements when x stays the same will be a crucial concept for this next lesson.


How Changing the Y-Value Affects Position

When plotting points on the coordinate plane, the x-value tells us how far to move left or right from the origin (the center of the grid), and the y-value tells us how far to move up or down. If we keep the x-value the same but change the y-value, we are moving along a straight vertical line. This movement can be visualized as climbing up or descending down a ladder that's fixed at one spot.

 

Examples of Plotting Points with Various Y-Values

Example 1: Plot the point (3, 4.5)

  • Step 1: Start at the origin (0, 0).
  • Step 2: Since the x-coordinate is 3, move 3 units to the right.
  • Step 3: Since the y-coordinate is 4.5, move 4.5 units upwards.
  • Result: You have plotted the point (3, 4.5), which is 3 units right of the y-axis and 4.5 units above the x-axis.


Example 2: Plot the point (3, -2)

  • Step 1: Start at the origin (0, 0).
  • Step 2: Move 3 units to the right because the x-coordinate is 3.
  • Step 3: Move 2 units downward because the y-coordinate is negative.
  • Result: You have now plotted the point (3, -2), which is directly below the first example but on the same vertical line (x=3).


Example 3: Plot the point (3, 0)

  • Step 1: Begin at the origin (0, 0).
  • Step 2: Move 3 units to the right, aligning with the previous points' x-coordinate.
  • Step 3: Do not move up or down as the y-coordinate is 0.
  • Result: The point (3, 0) lies directly on the x-axis, right where the vertical line passing through x=3 intersects it.


Understanding Vertical Movements

These examples demonstrate that changing the y-coordinate while keeping the x-coordinate constant results in movement along a vertical line at x=3. This line is called a vertical line test in mathematics, which helps us see the relationship between y-values and their vertical positions relative to the x-axis.


Conclusion

By focusing on changing only the y-values while keeping the x constant, we've seen how points move strictly vertically, either going up or climbing down the y-axis. This skill is important for understanding how graphs represent changes over time or any other situation where one quantity changes while another stays the same. Remember, every point on the coordinate plane tells a story, and by learning how to read these points, we become better at understanding the mathematical tales they tell.
