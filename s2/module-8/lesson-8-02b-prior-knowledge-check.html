📖 PRIOR KNOWLEDGE

In a previous lesson, we learned how to plot points on the coordinate plane using pairs of numbers called coordinates. In this lesson, we will be focusing on what happens when we change only the x-values while keeping the y-value the same. This specific way of moving along the coordinate plane shows us how to move horizontally, either left or right along the x-axis. Understanding how to manipulate the x-coordinate while keeping the y-coordinate constant is crucial for navigating this two-dimensional space effectively and will help us visualize data, solve problems, and understand patterns. In the next lesson, we will be exploring how to find the distance between two points when the y-value stays the same. Understanding the horizontal movements of points with the same y-value is a crucial skill for this concept.


How Changing the X-Value Affects Position

When plotting points on the coordinate plane, the x-value tells us how far to move left or right from the origin (the center of the grid), and the y-value tells us how far to move up or down. If we keep the y-value the same but change the x-value, we are moving along a straight horizontal line. This movement can be visualized as walking along a straight path that's fixed at one height.


Examples of Plotting Points with Various X-Values

Example 1: Plot the point (4.5, 3)

  • Step 1: Start at the origin (0, 0).
  • Step 2: Since the x-coordinate is 4.5, move 4.5 units to the right.
  • Step 3: Since the y-coordinate is 3, move 3 units upwards.
  • Result: You have plotted the point (4.5, 3), which is 4.5 units right of the y-axis and 3 units above the x-axis.


Example 2: Plot the point (-2, 3)

  • Step 1: Start at the origin (0, 0).
  • Step 2: Move 2 units to the left because the x-coordinate is negative.
  • Step 3: Move 3 units upwards because the y-coordinate is 3.
  • Result: You have now plotted the point (-2, 3), which is directly left of the first example but on the same horizontal line (y=3).


Example 3: Plot the point (0, 3)

  • Step 1: Begin at the origin (0, 0).
  • Step 2: Do not move left or right as the x-coordinate is 0.
  • Step 3: Move 3 units upwards to align with the previous points' y-coordinate.
  • Result: The point (0, 3) lies directly on the y-axis, right where the horizontal line passing through y=3 intersects it.


Understanding Horizontal Movements

These examples demonstrate that changing the x-coordinate while keeping the y-coordinate constant results in movement along a horizontal line at y=3. This line is called a horizontal line test in mathematics, which helps us see the relationship between x-values and their horizontal positions relative to the y-axis.


Conclusion

By focusing on changing only the x-values while keeping the y constant, we've seen how points move strictly horizontally, either going left or right on the x-axis. This skill is important for understanding how graphs represent changes over time or any other situation where one quantity changes while another stays the same. Remember, every point on the coordinate plane tells a story, and by learning how to read these points, we become better at understanding the mathematical tales they tell.
