📚 VOCABULARY

Area: The amount of space inside a shape, like the inside of a rectangle or circle. It's like counting how many square tiles it would take to completely cover a floor without any gaps.

Axes: The two lines that cross each other to form a coordinate plane. One is horizontal and called the x-axis, and the other is vertical and called the y-axis. They meet at a point called the origin.

Coordinates: Numbers that tell you the location of a point on a graph. The first number tells you how far to move right or left on the x-axis, and the second number tells you how far to move up or down on the y-axis.

Coordinate Plane: A flat surface where you can draw points, lines, and shapes using coordinates. It's divided into four parts by the axes, helping you describe exactly where things are placed.

Ordered Pair: A pair of numbers used to show the position of a point on the coordinate plane. The first number is for the x-axis, and the second is for the y-axis.

Origin: The starting point on the coordinate plane where the x-axis and y-axis cross. It's like the center point of a map and is always at (0, 0).

Perimeter: The total distance around the edge of a shape, like adding up all the sides of a triangle or a square. It's like walking all the way around a fence to measure how much fencing is needed.

Quadrant: One of the four sections of the coordinate plane, created by the crossing of the x-axis and y-axis. Each section is numbered from 1 to 4, starting from the upper right and moving counterclockwise.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to find the length and width of a rectangle on a coordinate plane and use that information to calculate the area and perimeter of the rectangle. This skill is invaluable in a variety of real-world and mathematical contexts, from architectural planning to simple everyday tasks like arranging furniture. Understanding these measurements on a coordinate plane enhances your spatial reasoning and helps in visualizing and solving practical problems effectively.


Finding Length and Width on a Coordinate Plane

To determine the dimensions of a rectangle on a coordinate plane, you must:

  1. Identify the coordinates of the rectangle's corners (vertices). Typically, you'll be given or can easily identify the vertices that lie along the same horizontal or vertical line (either the length or the width).
  2. Calculate the horizontal distance (width) between vertices that share the same y-coordinate.
  3. Calculate the vertical distance (length) between vertices that share the same x-coordinate.
  4. Use these distances to calculate the area and perimeter:
    • Area = Length × Width
    • Perimeter = 2 × (Length + Width)


Problem 1

  • Given: The coordinates of two opposite vertices of a rectangle are A(3, 2) and C(11, 7).

  • Task: Find the area and perimeter of the rectangle.
  • You can begin by plotting the other 2 vertices of the rectangle to visualize the length and width. They need to be in line with A and C.
  • So, B could go at (3, 7) and D could go at (11, 2)

  • Solution:
    • Length (horizontal distance) = |11 - 3| = 8 units
    • Width (vertical distance) = |7 - 2| = 5 units
    • Area = 8 × 5 = 40 square units
    • Perimeter = 2 × (8 + 5) = 26 units


Problem 2

  • Given: Vertices of a rectangle at B(4, -5) and D(-1, 3).
  • You can begin by creating your own graph and graphing the other 2 vertices to visualize the rectangle.
  • Point A would be at (4, 3) and C would be at (-1, -5)
  • Task: Calculate the perimeter and area of the rectangle.
  • Solution:
    • Length = |4 - (-1)| = 5 units
    • Width = |3 - (-5)| = 8 units
    • Area = 5 × 8 = 40 square units
    • Perimeter = 2 × (5 + 8) = 26 units
📋 REAL-WORLD PROBLEMS

Problem 3

  • Scenario: You are helping to plan a garden that fits perfectly between your house and the fence. The garden's corners near the house are at (2, 3) and the fence's corners are at (14, 10).
  • Task: Determine how much fencing is needed around the garden and how much area it will cover.
  • Solution:
    • Width = |14 - 2| = 12 units
    • Length = |10 - 3| = 7 units
    • Area = 12 × 7 = 84 square units
    • Perimeter = 2 × (12 + 7) = 38 units


Problem 4

  • Scenario: A school wants to create a new rectangular playground where one corner is at (-15, -5) and the opposite corner is at (-5, -10).
  • Task: Find the dimensions of the playground and calculate the area for landscaping and the perimeter for fencing.
  • Solution:
    • Width = |-5 - (-15)| = 10 units
    • Length = | -10 - (-5)| = 5 units
    • Area = 10 × 5 = 50 square units
    • Perimeter = 2 × (10 + 5) = 30 units
