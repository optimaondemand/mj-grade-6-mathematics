In a previous lesson, you learned how to find the five-number summary for a set of data in order to create a box plot. As we get ready to explore finding the five-number summary from a given box plot, it is important that we review what the five-number summary is and what each part represents.


What is a Box Plot?

A box plot is a graphical representation of a data set that shows the distribution of the data across five main points: the minimum, the first quartile (Q1), the median, the third quartile (Q3), and the maximum. These five points make up the five-number summary. Here’s what each part tells us:

  • Minimum: The smallest number in the data set.
  • Q1 (First Quartile): The median of the lower half of the data set. This is the 25th percentile.
  • Median: The middle value of the data set. This is the 50th percentile.
  • Q3 (Third Quartile): The median of the upper half of the data set. This is the 75th percentile.
  • Maximum: The largest number in the data set.


What is the Five-Number Summary?

The five-number summary is like the DNA of a data set, giving you a quick snapshot of its main characteristics. It includes:

  1. Minimum: The smallest number in the set.
  2. First Quartile (Q1): The middle number between the minimum and the median. It marks the 25th percentile, meaning 25% of the data is below this value.
  3. Median: The middle number of the set. If you line up all the numbers from smallest to largest, the median is the one right in the center.
  4. Third Quartile (Q3): The middle value between the median and the maximum. It marks the 75th percentile, with 75% of the data falling below this point.
  5. Maximum: The largest number in the set.

Conclusion

Box plots provide a clear visual of how your data is spread out, where it's centered, and if there are any outliers. The five-number summary helps us to summarize the data by looking at the minimum and maximum to show to full spread of the data, then looking at the minimum, middle, and maximum of the middle 50% of the data. This is what creates the box portion and helps to analyze what the middle 50% of the data shows us. By mastering box plots, you're adding a powerful tool to your mathematical toolkit.
