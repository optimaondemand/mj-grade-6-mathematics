📚 VOCABULARY

Central Tendency: This is a way to describe the middle of a group of numbers. It's like finding the best spot that represents where most of the numbers are hanging out.

Mean: The mean is what many people commonly refer to as the "average." You find it by adding up all the numbers in a list and then dividing by how many numbers there are. For example, if you have 5, 10, and 15, you add them up to get 30 and then divide by 3 (because there are 3 numbers), so the mean is 10.

Median: The median is the middle number in a list of numbers. To find it, you put all your numbers in order from smallest to largest. If you have an odd number of numbers, the median is the one right in the middle. If you have an even number of numbers, you take the two middle numbers, add them together, and divide by 2 to get the median.

Mode: The mode is the number that appears the most in a list of numbers. If you have a list of numbers and one number shows up more times than any other, that's the mode. Sometimes there might be more than one mode or none at all if no number repeats.

Numerical Data: This is information that is represented by numbers. For example, if you count how many apples are in a basket or measure how tall someone is, the numbers you get (like 10 apples, or 5 feet tall) are numerical data.

Range: The range tells you how spread out your numbers are. You find it by taking the largest number in your list and subtracting the smallest number. If your smallest number is 1 and your largest is 10, then the range is 9 (because 10 - 1 = 9). This shows how far apart the biggest and smallest numbers are.

Statistical Measures: These are different ways to summarize or describe a bunch of numbers. Mean, median, mode, and range are all examples of statistical measures. They help us understand what's typical for a set of numbers or how they're spread out.

Variability: This term describes how much your numbers differ from each other. If all your numbers are close together, there's low variability. But if they're all spread out and very different, there's high variability. It's a way of talking about how much the numbers change.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we're going to explore two very important concepts that help us understand numbers better: the mean and the median. These are types of "central tendency," which is a fancy way of saying they help us find the center or the average spot in a bunch of numbers. Let's dive into what these terms mean and how we can use them in real-life situations!

Let's begin by reminding ourselves what the mean and median are.


What is Mean?

The mean is just another word for the average. You can find it by adding up all the numbers in your list and then dividing by how many numbers there are. It gives us a good idea of what's typical for our data, or where the middle ground is.

What is Median?

The median is the middle number in a list that's been arranged from smallest to largest. If there's an even number of items in the list, then the median is the average of the two middle numbers. The median is super useful because it shows us the exact middle point of our data, even if some numbers are really big or small.


Why Are The Mean and Median Important?

The mean and median give us clues about the data. The mean shows us the overall level, but it can be influenced by very high or very low numbers. The median gives us a middle point that isn't affected by extreme values, which can be helpful in understanding what's normal or typical in the data.

Let's explore some examples.


Example 1

Situation: A local ice cream shop keeps track of how many ice creams are sold each day for two weeks. The numbers are: 12, 15, 9, 13, 14, 17, 11, 10, 16, 12, 14, 15, 13, 18.

Question: What are the mean and median sales, and what do they tell us about ice cream sales over these two weeks?

Solution:

  • Finding the mean
    • To find the mean, add all the numbers together and divide by the number of days sales were tracked.
  • Finding the median
    • To find the median, arrange the numbers in ascending order and find the middle number.

The mean tells us the average number of ice creams sold in a day is 13.5. The median tells us that the typical number of ice cream sales is 13.5 as well. Since these two numbers are identical, the data set is fairly uniform, with no skewness or outliers that would affect the average number.


Example 2

Situation: A group of friends tracks how many books they read over the summer: 8, 7, 2, 3, 6, 3, 10, 7, 8, 11, 8, 9, 9, 2.

Question: What are the mean and median number of books read, and what do these measures tell us about the friends' reading habits? Round to the nearest hundredths if necessary.

Solution:

  • Finding the mean
    • Add all the numbers together and divide by the number of friends. Since we divide by 14, we will need to round the number. 
  • Finding the median
    • Arrange the numbers in ascending order and find the middle number.

The mean tells us the average number of books read is about 6.64. The median tells us that the typical number of books read was 7.5. These numbers are off. Telling us that our data is skewed somewhat. There were a couple who only read 2 or 3 books when we know the most typical number of books read was between 7 and 8 since our median is 7.5.


Example 3

Situation: In a local marathon, the finishing times (in hours) for participants were: 4.5, 3.9, 4.0, 4.3, 3.8, 4.3, 4.4, 4.1, 3.7, 3.6, 9.0.

Question: Calculate and interpret the mean and median marathon times. What do these tell us about the participants' performance?

Solution:

  • Finding the mean
    • Add all the numbers together and divide by the number of participants. Since we divide by 11, we will need to round the number. Round off to the nearest hundredths.
  • Finding the median
    • Arrange the numbers in ascending order and find the middle number.

The mean tells us that the average finishing time was 5.22, showing the overall pace of the runners. This number is slightly higher than the typical finishing time from the median of 4.1. This could be related to the one time that is much higher than the majority, pulling the value of the mean up. The mean is affected by outliers or skewed data, whereas the median is not as affected.


Conclusion

These examples show that the mean and median help us understand different aspects of our data. The mean gives a general idea, while the median helps us see what's typical when we ignore the extremes. Whether it's ice cream sales, summer reading, or marathon times, these measures of central tendency can tell us a lot about what's going on in the world around us. Happy calculating!
